# The coordinator's exam and his stated emphases

Source: the pre-exam review meeting (מפגש חזרה) he held over Zoom, plus a past
exam of his. Everything here is in his own framing — when writing questions on
these subjects, prefer his terminology and his distinctions over a textbook's.

## What he said about the exam itself

- 50 questions: about 12 open (fill-in) and 38 multiple choice.
- Open answers are **one word to three**, not full sentences: *"לא צריך תשובה
  מלאה כמו שלימדו אותנו בבית ספר... רק בקצרה"*. He marks them by hand.
- Sat in a classroom, closed book, no laptop. He is explicit that he does not
  set take-home exams: *"כולנו בני אדם ויש לנו יצרים"*.
- Questions are reused and adapted from past ministry and course exams, so the
  *shape* of the question matters as much as the fact.
- The syllabus is the Ministry of Tourism's, identical across courses; what
  varies between coordinators is emphasis, not scope.

## The habit he drilled hardest

Read every word of the question and every word of every answer, to the end. He
gave two of his own questions as the model:

1. **`שמה של אחת מחמש ערי הפלישתים`** — with `תל קסילה` among the options.
   Tel Qasile is a genuine Philistine site with a Philistine temple; it is not
   one of the five cities. *"שימו לב למילים בשאלה, לכל מילה ומילה."*

2. **`בית ארבעת המרחבים הוא:`** — one wrong option begins *"מבנה מתקופת
   המלוכה"*, which is true, and then adds *"ובו שני מרחבים בקומה הראשונה
   ושניים בקומה השנייה"*, which is false. The correct option also opens with
   "מתקופת המלוכה". Only the ending separates them. *"אבל תמשיכו לקרוא עד
   הסוף."*

He also warned about options that are right-sounding but off-axis — `דרום מישור
החוף` offered where the expected answer is the geographic origin of the
Philistines: *"זה לא סתם שמו פה דרום מישור החוף. זה משהו שבא לבלבל."*

## The four exam subjects

תקופת הברזל · נצרות · יהדות · גבולות ואזורי ארץ ישראל

### תקופת הברזל

- **Periodisation**: ברזל א' 1200–1000 = ההתנחלות = ספר שופטים; ברזל ב'
  1000–586 = תקופת המלוכה. ישראל נופלת 722, יהודה 586. He accepts 722 or 720
  for Samaria and says not to agonise: *"הכותרת — חורבן ישראל 722"*.
- **כתובת תל דן**, 1993 — the first extra-biblical mention of בית דוד. Written
  by an Aramean king (Hazael) who describes killing two kings; the same two
  names as the Jehu story, יורם מלך ישראל and אחזיהו מלך יהודה. Before 1993 the
  minimalists (ישראל פינקלשטיין) had no counter-evidence.
- **חורבת קייאפה** — excavated by גרפינקל וגנור; carbon-14 on olive pits dates
  it to the early 10th century; a two-gated fortified city opposite תל עזקה; a
  bone assemblage with almost no non-kosher animals, which is the argument that
  it is Judahite rather than Philistine.
- **בית ארבעת המרחבים** — three rooms around a courtyard, typical of the
  monarchy; also found among Philistines, Phoenicians and in Transjordan.
- **ירבעם** splits the kingdom and immediately founds two cult sites, דן ובית אל.
- **חזקיהו** — the Broad Wall, first to enclose הר ציון; נקבת השילוח; קנקני
  למלך; a religious reform. Revolted against Assyria relying on Egypt, *"משענת
  קנה רצוץ"*. Sennacherib arrives 701, destroys Lachish, fails at Jerusalem.
- **Two reforming kings**: חזקיהו ויאשיהו. The altar-turned-latrine at Lachish
  is attributed to Hezekiah; the massebot at Arad to Josiah.
- **עוזיהו** fortified Jerusalem and built desert forts, but no walls of his are
  archaeologically known — he corrected a student on this.
- **תבליטי לכיש מול חרסי לכיש** — he flagged this twice as the confusion to
  avoid. The reliefs are Assyrian, 701, from Sennacherib's palace at Nineveh.
  The ostraca are Babylonian, 586, and carry *"לא רואים את עזקה"*.
- **פלישתים** — part of גויי הים. The Bible says כפתור; research says Crete and
  the Aegean. Five cities: אשדוד, אשקלון, עזה, גת, עקרון (three still exist,
  two are tells). סרנים are their rulers. תל קסילה is Philistine but not one of
  the five.
- **מסע שישק 925** — the first point where an extra-biblical find meets the
  biblical text in this country, and therefore the anchor that dates everything
  around it. About five years after the 930 split, in the reign of רחבעם.
- **אבן העזר** — the ark brought from שילה, the Philistines win, archaeology
  shows a destruction layer at Shiloh that the text does not mention; the ark
  goes to בית שמש and then קריית יערים.
- **ההתנחלות** — Adam Zertal's survey of הר מנשה; hundreds of small highland
  villages; silos, elliptical plans, collar-rim jars, near-zero non-kosher
  bones. עזבת צרטה near Rosh HaAyin is identified with Even HaEzer.

### נצרות

Iconoclasm; flat Orthodox icons against Catholic statues; the first miracle at
כפר קנה; the life of Jesus in order with its sites (נצרת, עין כרם, בית לחם,
הירדן, הר הקפיצה, כפר נחום, הר האושר, בית עניה, בית פאגי, הר ציון, גת שמנים,
בית קייפא, פילטוס, אמאוס, בניאס, הר תבור, הר הזיתים); the three cursed towns
כורזים, בית צידה, כפר נחום; פנטקוסט fifty days on הר ציון; פטרוס — the Jaffa
vision, קורנליוס, the keys, the inverted cross; פאולוס as the real theologian;
the sacraments, baptism common to all and two of seven for Lutherans; a
different canon, book order and chapter division.

### יהדות

Which festivals are biblical — Hanukkah is not, Purim is (and note the third
tier: Purim is from the Tanakh but not from the Torah); ראש השנה appears as
יום תרועה; the Shema in tefillin, mezuzah and ספר דברים, and *not* in קדיש,
עשרת הדיברות or שמונה עשרה; the four fasts — י' בטבת the start of the siege,
י"ז בתמוז the breach, ט' באב both Temples, צום גדליה ג' בתשרי — against יום
כיפור as the only Torah fast; צפת in the 16th century, Kabbalah and the
Shulchan Aruch; the Mishnah (רבי יהודה הנשיא, ~200 CE, ציפורי, buried בית
שערים); the two Talmuds (Babylonian in Babylon, Jerusalem in Tiberias);
משנה תורה and ארבעה טורים; vocalisation in Tiberias *after* the Talmud; 613
mitzvot; Nisan as the first month and the Babylonian month names; סת"ם; the
Tanakh sealed around 200 BCE (against the Mishnah around 200 CE); יוחנן בן זכאי
and *"תן לי יבנה וחכמיה"*; and chronological ordering of figures — רבי עקיבא,
רבי יהודה הנשיא, בנימין מטודלה, יוסף קארו.

### גבולות ואזורי ארץ ישראל

כינרות is **not** one of the Lower Galilee valleys (נטופה, סכנין, תורען are);
חמרה → the Sharon; כורכר → the coastal plain; the Negev's northern boundary is
the **200 mm isohyet**, not Beer Sheva — he was explicit that the expected
answer is the climatic line; rain shadow against the global desert belt as two
distinct causes of aridity; עמק יזרעאל between רכס נצרת and the Samarian hills
and Gilboa; בקעת בית הכרם divides Upper from Lower Galilee; the Sharon runs
from הירקון in the south to נחל תנינים in the north; נחל צין ונחל פארן in the
Negev and **נחל סער** on the Hermon; עיבל the mount of curse and גריזים of
blessing.

## The third block: בית שני, רומית, ביזנטית, ארכיאולוגיה קלאסית

For the exam after the four subjects above he named בית שני, התקופה הרומית,
התקופה הביזנטית and ארכיאולוגיה קלאסית. He said the required knowledge is
Eyal Miron's online lectures (Haifa Moodle); his own summaries add order and
timelines; Erez Bratman's Jerusalem tours (City of David, the pilgrims' road,
the Western Wall tunnels, the Herodian quarter) are review for the same
material. Bat-Zion Ziv's "הגדרת מושגים" list for each unit is the syllabus —
every concept on it deserves a question. Exams 11–18 (`TEMPLE_EXAMS`, built
by `tools/build_temple_exams.py`) cover it; the NotebookLM notebooks for the
three units and the two tour days are the source.

Facts the lectures state that differ from common shortcuts: no mass exile in
70 (the decline was gradual); "דרך עולי הרגל" was paved under the procurators
(Pilate coins under the paving), not by Herod; Aristobulus I, not Yannai, was
the first Hasmonean "king"; the Byzantine period is not a conquest — it is the
same empire turning Christian.

## Using this file

Treat it as his emphasis list, not as a closed scope — he said new questions
can appear, but the directions and subjects will be the ones already asked.
When the user gives fresh material for a new exam, that material governs; this
file governs the house style, the traps, and the subjects it already covers.

### Review session before the Second Temple–Byzantine exam (Zoom, October 2026)

Exams 16–18 are built on it. What he stressed:

- **Eyal Miron's films decide** where a source conflicts with another lecturer: the Ministry approved them. Second Temple starts at **Cyrus's declaration (538)**, the "gong"; the exile years (586–538) are learnt briefly, chiefly **Gedaliah ben Ahikam** (he has a fast day). The Byzantine period ends with the **conquest of Jerusalem (638)**; Yarmuk (636) is the decisive battle on the way.
- **Jerusalem's walls by Josephus's count**: first — Hasmonean (Nehemiah only repaired breaches, in 52 days); second — attributed to Herod, course unknown, Golgotha outside it; third — Agrippa I (41–44), finished by the rebels, first to fall; then Eudocia (5th c.).
- **Aqueducts**: lower to the Temple Mount via Wilson's arch, for the Temple's needs; upper to Herod's palace; Arub/Biar feed Solomon's Pools. Robinson's arch = the stair to the Royal Stoa.
- **Second Temple synagogues**: Torah reading and assembly, **no prayer, no orientation, no figures** (Theodotus, Magdala, Gamla, Herodium, Masada). Byzantine synagogues: zodiacs (Sepphoris, Beit Alfa, Hammat Tiberias); Capernaum in white limestone.
- **People in order**: Alexander → Diadochi → Syrian wars → Antiochus III (good) / IV (bad) → Hasmoneans (battles on the roads to Jerusalem) → Pompey 63 → Antipater, Herod → Archelaus deposed 6 CE → Pilate → Agrippa I → Florus → revolt began in Caesarea (Jews vs gentiles at a synagogue) → Vespasian/Titus, Berenice → Hadrian (spelt with ה or א) → Diocletian (limes) → Constantine → Julian → Theodosius (state religion; Nicaea was doctrinal) → Heraclius → 638.
- **Revolts**: Great Revolt 66–73, Diaspora 115–117, Bar Kokhba 132–135 (source: Cassius Dio; whether Jerusalem was taken is **unknown**), Gallus 351 (+363 earthquake).
- **Classical architecture**: the parts of the order and the entablature **by heart** (Vitruvius; Absalom's tomb).

Exam 18 is made of the Ministry's own questions on these subjects: the official-key sittings first, then the 2016–19 sittings and Efrat Nakash's 2000–2017 collection, each answer checked. The explanation names the sitting.
